Before you hire us

Six questions to review before approving a project.

Use these questions in your first conversation. They are a guide to the details your written proposal must confirm; ownership transfers, support coverage and commercial terms are agreed for each project.

Who owns the source code, website and business data?

Ask the proposal to name the contracting business and specify rights to custom code, designs, content and business data. Confirm any transfer conditions, third-party licence limits and whether you receive repository access, exports or both. Domain, hosting and supplier accounts need named owners.

Ask to record: Ownership schedule: assets, rights, account owners and transfer conditions.

What access and documentation will be handed over?

Agree an access inventory, deployment responsibilities, administrator access, documentation, training and backup or export instructions. Decide how credentials are transferred securely and who confirms receipt. Handover items depend on the agreed scope.

Ask to record: Handover checklist: accounts, documentation, training and recovery responsibilities.

How are milestones, approvals and payments agreed?

Record deliverables, acceptance checks, review dates and the person authorised to approve each milestone. Confirm price, currency, taxes, payment triggers and external charges in writing. Scope changes need agreement on cost and timing before proceeding.

Ask to record: Milestone schedule: deliverables, approval owner, payment trigger and change process.

What support and maintenance are included?

Ask for the coverage period, support channels and hours, response expectations, escalation contact and charges. Distinguish defect fixes from enhancements, provider changes and recurring maintenance. Confirm renewal and cancellation arrangements in the proposal or support agreement.

Ask to record: Support schedule: coverage, exclusions, contacts, charges and renewal terms.

How can I check company records?

Request the legal name and records for the business that will contract and invoice your project. Match the supplied registration details to the applicable official record. Website photos and published profiles alone do not establish registration credentials. Verified credentials remain pending supplied records.

Ask to record: Company records: contracting name and applicable registration documents.

Can I review relevant work or speak to a project reference?

Explore the published project records and ask which work matches your requirements. Request a customer reference through BANDEVI; introductions and customer details depend on permission. Fictional demos explain workflows, and do not represent a customer result or endorsement.

Ask to record: Project evidence: relevant delivery scope and a customer-approved reference, when available.

Five stages to agree before you begin

1. Requirements and written scope

Describe your business problem, users and current tools. Agree the first release, deliverables, exclusions, dependencies, milestones and acceptance criteria in the proposal.

Customer review: approve the scope and commercial terms before work starts.

2. Design review and approval

Review the proposed pages, screens and customer journey. Consolidate feedback through the agreed project contact and record approved design decisions.

Customer review: confirm the design and identify any changes to scope.

3. Development and testing

Review agreed milestones and test the workflows against the acceptance criteria. Confirm supplier access and third-party requirements for any integrations.

Customer review: check the agreed features, record issues and approve acceptance when resolved.

4. Launch and handover

Agree the launch checklist, access responsibilities, migration needs and release approval. Record domain, source-code and data ownership alongside hosting, documentation and training deliverables.

Customer review: approve launch and confirm receipt of the agreed handover items.

5. Support arrangements

Confirm support channels, hours, response expectations and the maintenance work included. Agree how new requests and third-party changes are handled.

Customer review: keep the support scope and renewal terms in writing.

Keep decisions clear

  • Name the customer approver and project contact.
  • Record milestone decisions and outstanding questions.
  • Agree the effect of scope changes on price and timing before proceeding.
  • Confirm payment triggers, third-party costs and acceptance terms.
