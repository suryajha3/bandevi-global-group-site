Delivery path

A simple path from idea to working system.

01

Map one workflow

Your process owner reviews the current steps, inputs, repeated work and desired outcome. The output is a workflow map with a named owner for each decision.

02

Agree triggers and permissions

Review the source of each trigger, access rules, approval points and missing-data or retry behaviour. Record provider access and integration dependencies before including a connection.

03

Rehearse the first release

Use representative test records to review a normal task, a repeated trigger, a restricted user and an unresolved exception. Agree the acceptance evidence before rollout.

04

Hand over monitoring and ownership

Review the agreed deployment, recovery checks, documentation and staff training. Name the person responsible for alerts, failed tasks and future changes.

Plan a reviewable first release

What to agree before development

Bring one repeated task and an anonymised record. Agree the proposed outputs below before committing to a build.

Workflow map

The trigger, source record, decisions, owners and end state.

Rules and access matrix

Who can view, approve, change, retry or resolve each action.

Integration-readiness note

Available APIs or exports, permissions, identifiers, limits, fees and the authoritative system for each record.

Acceptance set

A normal case, repeated event, missing reference, restricted user and failed update with the expected result.

Handover plan

Alert ownership, operating instructions, access, recovery and the agreed support scope.

These are proposed artefacts to agree in discovery, not a free audit, a fixed-price package or a claim that every item is included automatically. The written proposal confirms deliverables, dependencies, fees and milestones.

Illustrative travel workflow

Keep a failed update visible until the right person resolves it

This fictional planning example describes proposed scope using a booking-related update that cannot be matched to a source record. It does not demonstrate a connected API, deployed retry engine or customer result. Confirm what the selected tools can support and test the agreed cases before rollout.

  1. Retain the failed update. The proposed workflow would keep the source reference and failure reason for fictional booking EXAMPLE-01.
  2. Route a review task. The workflow would assign the unmatched item to the agreed owner. A repeated event should not create another task.
  3. Require the right decision. A missing reference, changed approval or uncertain outcome needs a visible review decision. Only a resolved, verified match should permit the next agreed action.
  4. Agree the retry boundary. A temporary technical failure may be eligible for a limited retry. Retrying an update is not authority to approve a payment, send a customer message or mark work complete.
  5. Report the reviewed state. Agree how the last checked record, decision and next action would be shown. A review task or payment-status record does not establish a transfer or bank settlement.

Provider access, available events, permissions, retry rules and any responsibility for moving money must be assessed and agreed in the proposal. No bank, payment or messaging integration is implied by this example.

Choose Travel ERP finance when the main requirement is booking-level customer and supplier records. Choose automation when existing systems remain in place and events, decisions and exceptions need to pass between them. The two can be scoped together after discovery.

Business automation FAQ

Questions to settle before a first release.

Use these questions to agree a useful starting scope, dependencies and review responsibilities.

What is business automation?

Business automation uses workflows, reminders, routing, notifications, dashboards, and system rules to reduce repeated manual work across sales, operations, finance, support, and reporting.

Does BANDEVI GLOBAL GROUP build business automation systems?

Yes. BANDEVI builds automation for lead routing, follow-ups, CRM, ERP, WhatsApp and email notifications, document requests, task queues, approvals, portals, and dashboards.

Can automation connect with CRM and ERP?

Yes. Automation can connect with CRM and ERP workflows for lead assignment, pipeline updates, tasks, approvals, invoices, payments, documents, reporting, and team notifications.

Can BANDEVI automate WhatsApp and customer communication?

Yes. BANDEVI can plan WhatsApp and communication workflows for enquiries, follow-ups, document requests, service status, payment reminders, booking notes, and support updates.

Which businesses can use automation?

Travel companies, service businesses, e-commerce brands, food-product brands, consulting teams, multi-office groups, and internal operations teams can use automation to reduce missed work and improve visibility.
